# Selective Hearing: Ranked Technical Approaches

*Synthesis of ~360 literature surveys, 11 October 2026. British English.*

## Executive summary (one page)

**The task:** pick the target speaker's voice out of a two-speaker mixture on a single phone microphone, streaming, on a phone CPU. The minimum bar is relaxed and honest: the system must select the correct speaker at **0 dB SIR** (target and interferer equally loud); working at **−5 dB** is good; **−15 dB** is aspirational.

**The headline finding:** the 0 dB bar is reachable with *already-published* pieces. Nothing in the literature does enrollment-conditioned extraction at −15 dB SIR on phone hardware, streaming — that regime remains our experiment, and should be treated as research upside, not a demo requirement. The gap between 0 dB and −15 dB is where nearly every surveyed mechanism breaks; the honest move is to build the 0 dB system first, instrument it with confusion metrics, and let the −15 dB work be a measured research programme rather than a demo promise.

**What converges across 8 independent mining passes:**

1. **The identity gate is the load-bearing instrument.** Enrollment↔output cosine similarity as a *veto/admission gate* — never an optimised reward — plus residual-correlation (CSE) as the "separation happened" leg, plus subtract-recover retry. This is the cheapest, fastest, most transferable thing in the whole corpus and it directly implements the minimum bar.
2. **Conditioning geometry matters more than encoder size.** Four independent lines agree the pooled speaker embedding is the bottleneck, not the encoder: frame-level enrollment↔mixture cross-attention (USEF-TSE, TASLP 2025), TF-Map per-frame similarity cues (+2.08 dB), de-pooled ECAPA features. The project's frozen ECAPA is fine (T-307: 99.1%); the *conditioning* is impoverished.
3. **All the cheap wins are training-side, zero inference cost.** Asymmetric-SIR training distributions, enrollment augmentation, confusion-aware losses (mean-of-dB SI-SDR, asymmetric deletion penalties, X-SepFormer chunk reweighting, SC-TSE consistency), nontarget anti-laundering training, SPCL curricula. Build these before touching architecture.
4. **Rank by measured phone runtime, never MACs.** Two published in-domain MAC↔wall-time inversions; aggregate RTF<1 is inadmissible (a published model passed RTF<1 while missing all ~12,000 frame deadlines). Per-frame deadline histograms, mean/p99, on the actual SoC.
5. **Dual-mic machinery is dead for the demo** (one mic confirmed). MVDR, AuxIVA chains, beam banks, reference-mic gating — all demoted to post-demo. Visual/lip-reading likewise. This killed roughly a third of the surveyed volume; what remains is single-channel and the ranking below reflects that.

**Top 5 demo candidates** (see §2 for build sketches): (A) the identity-gated causal TSE core — the composition everything below feeds; (B) VoiceFilter-Lite-style filterbank fast path — fastest shipped precedent; (C) denoiser-guided dual-path D-LGTSE on pretrained GTCRN; (D) TF-MLPNet+2F tiny core + TF-Map conditioning + confusion loss stack; (E) interferer-first masker + TSE-on-residue.

## 1. Ranked approaches (best to worst)

*Ranking order: (a) likelihood of correct speaker selection at 0 dB SIR, single-mic, streaming; (b) graceful behaviour at −5 dB; (c) hackathon build speed; (d) upside toward −15 dB.*

### 1. Runtime identity gate + confusion-recovery kit (gate, veto, retry)

The single most load-bearing mechanism in the corpus. Per 1–2 s window: compute enrollment↔output embedding cosine (frozen ECAPA/WeSpeaker; REAL-TSE's official SpkSim metric validates the leg at challenge scale) **and** residual correlation corr(output, mixture−output) as the parameter-free "separation happened" check (CSE). On a failed gate: flag/veto — never adapt statistics, never update banks, never admit pseudo-labels. Retry layer: subtract the wrong output from the mixture and re-extract (Zhao TCP post-filter, >1 dB on confused samples, zero retraining). Thresholds calibrated per SIR bin on enrollment-swap probes; Malek's speech–speech false-positive numbers (5.5% significant / 10.3% raw — the 0.3% headline is speech–noise only) are the calibration prior. **Papers:** Zhao et al., Interspeech 2022, arXiv:2204.01355; Malek et al., arXiv:2111.03482; Neri & Braun, ICASSP 2023, arXiv:2303.07569; REAL-TSE overview, arXiv:2607.15198. **SIR:** 0 dB ✓ (the minimum-bar instrument); −5 dB with recalibration; −15 dB is the load-bearing precondition, not a solver. **Why #1:** directly implements the relaxed minimum bar; zero architecture change; every other approach's failure mode is "wrong speaker extracted", and this is the only mechanism that *detects* that. **Risk:** embedding geometry shifts under interferer dominance — uncalibrated thresholds false-veto real speech. **Difficulty:** easy (days).

### 2. Direct enrollment-conditioned causal TSE — the composition core

The demo system itself: a causal separator core (TF-MLPNet+2F-class tiny topology, or SkiM skip-memory) conditioned on enrollment via **TF-Map per-frame similarity cues + multi-layer FiLM/frame-level cross-attention** (never pooled-vector-only), trained on an asymmetric-SIR distribution U(−15,+15) dB with the confusion-aware loss stack (approach 16), wrapped at runtime by the identity gate (approach 1). This is the composition the whole literature converges on: direct conditioning beats separation-then-select (MUSE's −1.3 dB collapse is the structural warning), frame-level conditioning beats pooled embeddings (USEF-TSE TASLP 2025 SOTA 23.3 dB), and the gate handles what training can't. **Papers:** USEF-TSE, arXiv:2409.02615 (TASLP 2025); TF-MLPNet+2F, arXiv:2508.03047; Itani/Chen/Gollakota; TF-Map (SOND lineage); SkiM, arXiv:2201.10800. **SIR:** 0 dB ✓ by construction; −5 dB expected; −15 dB unmeasured anywhere — the composition is our experiment. **Why #2:** highest probability of a working streaming demo; every sub-mechanism has published evidence at 0 dB. **Risk:** the −15 dB transfer of cross-attention (attention latching onto the louder K/V) is the open experiment. **Difficulty:** medium (weeks).

### 3. VoiceFilter-Lite-style filterbank-domain enrollment TSE

Masking on stacked log-Mel features rather than waveform; asymmetric loss penalising over-suppression; FiLM conditioning that shrinks the model (3.47→3.23 MB); 130 KB personal-VAD gate running the heavy extractor only on target-active frames; INT8 streaming Conformer in PVAD 2.0. This is the *only* enrollment-conditioned extraction family with measured on-device numbers — the closest thing to a shipped precedent. Optimised for ASR WER rather than fidelity, but the architecture, the asymmetric loss, and the PVAD-gated compute discipline all transfer. **Papers:** Wang et al., Interspeech 2020, arXiv:2009.04323; Rikhye et al., ICASSP 2022, arXiv:2107.01201; Personal VAD 2.0, arXiv:2204.03793. **SIR:** 0 dB ✓ (production); negative SIR unevaluated. **Why #3:** fastest credible path to *something demoable*; production lineage de-risks the engineering. **Risk:** optimises WER, not confusion rate; confusion never stratified by SIR. **Difficulty:** easy (recipe is public).

### 4. Enrollment augmentation + asymmetric-SIR training discipline

The cheapest confusion reducers in the corpus, all training-side: corrupt the **enrollment branch** (MUSAN noise + FRAM-RIR, β≈0.6, band widened to U(−15,15) dB, clean-first curriculum); SSA self-estimated-speech augmentation (+2.1–2.5 dB, one extra forward pass); pseudo-speaker hard samples (resampling + WSOLA — NSR halved 2.35%→1.10%, stacks with triplet metric learning); enrollment span-masking (θ≈30% on the *cue*, never the mixture); two-enrollment consistency regulariser (DSEF-PNet, zero inference cost). Pair with the data discipline: set mixture levels by **loudness difference** (LKFS-based SIR, never RMS), audit the SIR histogram of real data (real recordings are inevitably SIR-imbalanced), train U(−15,+15) dB — no published TSE paper trains asymmetrically, and every benchmark uses symmetric SIR, which is precisely the deployment mismatch. **Papers:** Li et al., SLT 2024, arXiv:2409.09589; You et al., arXiv:2505.21805; DSEF-PNet, arXiv:2505.12288; ITU-R BS.1770-4; EARS, arXiv:2406.06185. **SIR:** 0 dB ✓; −5 dB primary lever; −15 dB needs the widened band. **Why #4:** dataloader-only changes, hours to days, and it attacks the identity path directly. **Risk:** SSA self-poisoning at −15 dB (first-pass estimate latches onto the interferer); never SpecAugment on enrollment (raises accuracy while lowering SI-SDR). **Difficulty:** easy.

### 5. Frame-level enrollment↔mixture cross-attention (embedding-free early fusion)

Drop the pooled speaker vector as the conditioning bottleneck: cache the enrollment's frame-level features once (INDPROMPT discipline — precomputed at enrollment, never looks back at the mixture, the only prompt interface with published causalization, FasTUSS −1.8 dB tax) and let the mixture attend to them inside the separator blocks; or TF-Map: per-frame cosine similarity mixture-frames↔enrollment-frames → softmax over enrollment → target-anchored cue (+2.08 dB SI-SDRi, +4.75 pp accuracy, causal-friendly). Direction of travel across five independent lines is away from pooled embeddings; DCF-Net's 0.4% confusion (preprint, arXiv:2502.08191 — *not* IRA's number) and SEF-PNet's 11.62→13.00 dB encoder-free gain say the same thing. Keep a frozen global identity vote in every frame, or attention latches onto phonetic similarity — i.e. the louder interferer at −15 dB (FragmentVC anti-pattern). **Papers:** USEF-TSE, arXiv:2409.02615; SEF-PNet, arXiv:2501.11274 (ICASSP 2025); SoloSpeech, arXiv:2505.19314; WHYV/GTB&GTF, arXiv:2410.00527 (+3.1 dB over FiLM, zero-shot unseen speakers *and* languages — the best −15 dB transfer candidate of any interface). **SIR:** 0 dB ✓; −5 dB plausible; −15 dB open risk. **Why #5:** the conditioning upgrade with the most convergent evidence; GTB&GTF is the single best literature bet for −15 dB. **Risk:** no phone-CPU RTF anywhere in the family; latent-space fusion erases the scalar gate surface — gate must live outside as veto. **Difficulty:** medium.

### 6. Denoiser-guided dual-path TSE (D-LGTSE)

Frozen streaming GTCRN denoises the mixture → Y_d; the enrollment↔mixture context attention reads **Y_d** (guidance decontamination: noise contaminates guidance weights, so strip it before attention); the extraction backbone masks the **raw** mixture (distortion never baked into output); distortion robustness handled in training (D-arm: denoised-mixture augmentation — offline 8.32 dB > on-the-fly 8.10 > concat 7.96). Never cascade denoiser→extractor on the waveform alone. Pretrained MIT streaming GTCRN weights exist. **Papers:** Huang et al., arXiv:2508.19583; TripleC, arXiv:2512.04945 (w=50, zero-inference-cost); GTCRN, Rong et al., ICASSP 2024. **SIR:** 0/−5 dB fit; at −15 dB the front-end aggressiveness knob (Carson's dual-asymmetric λ) must be re-tuned per SIR bin — an aggressive denoiser erases the quiet target. **Why #6:** copyable three-script recipe with public weights; decontaminates guidance exactly where the 0 dB bar is hardest. **Risk:** guidance weights computed on a target-less Y_d are pure poison. **Difficulty:** easy–medium.

### 7. Confusion-first evaluation + deadline-histogram runtime protocol

The measurement discipline everything else is judged by: report **NSR** (fraction of utterances with SI-SDRi ≤ 0), **r_scr** (chunk-level confusion rate, 250 ms/125 ms), **TSOS** (over-suppression), all stratified by SIR bin {−12,−6,0,+6,+12} dB, medians never means; wrong-enrollment sanity row in every eval (wrong d-vector must perform *worse* than no enhancement); never report SI-SDRi pooled across strata (it inflates from negative baselines and hides wrong-speaker extraction). Runtime: never quote RTF without runtime×silicon×build; measure per-frame **paced** to the real frame deadline (racing misstates per-frame cost 4.2×); report mean/p99/miss-rate — aggregate RTF<1 is inadmissible (published model passed throughput while missing all ~12,000 deadlines). Budget the causal core at ≤1 GFLOP/s. **Papers:** X-SepFormer, arXiv:2303.05023 (r_scr, −14.8% rel SC errors); You et al., arXiv:2505.21805 (NSR); Eskimez et al., ICASSP 2022, arXiv:2110.09625 (TSOS); faster-enhancer.c, arXiv:2607.25350. **SIR:** all — this is the instrument. **Why #7:** a hackathon demo that can't *measure* confusion can't claim the 0 dB bar; this is days of eval code that de-risks everything else. **Risk:** none as a tool; the risk is not using it. **Difficulty:** easy.

### 8. Interferer-first extraction (explicit undesired branch + mutual exclusivity)

At negative SIR the interferer's mask is the *easier* estimation problem (louder, dominant energy): estimate the interferer explicitly — as a negative enrollment ("who the target is NOT", +2.1 dB SI-SNRi, Xu et al. causal), as a parallel undesired branch with reverse attention and mutual-exclusivity penalty (SEANet: 8.70M beats 15.01M MuSE), or as NASS's additional-noise-output + patch-wise contrastive learning (+1–2 dB, <0.1M params) — then extract the target from the residual or condition on the interferer estimate. Zhao's post-filtering subtraction is the zero-retraining runtime version. This is the team's own interferer-first thesis, and the loudness asymmetry may compensate for the guidance asymmetry — but it is an argument, not evidence: the falsification gate is a half-day CPU oracle test (frozen TSE on residue vs direct). **Papers:** Xu et al., NeurIPS 2025, arXiv:2502.16611; SEANet, arXiv:2404.18501; NASS, ICASSP 2024, arXiv:2305.10761; Zhao et al., arXiv:2204.01355; research/interferer-first-*.md. **SIR:** −5/−15 dB by design; predicted neutral at +6 dB and above. **Why #8:** the highest-upside −15 dB idea with a cheap falsification path; directly attacks confusion. **Risk:** error propagation — a wrong interferer estimate corrupts the residue; NASS's contrastive is identity-blind and can suppress the target too. **Difficulty:** medium (zero-cost runtime post-filter first; trained variant second).

### 9. Gated IRA-style embedding refinement

Feed the extraction latent back through the auxiliary network to refine the speaker embedding (aₙ = aₙ₋₁ + μ·A(x̂ₙ₋₁)): +1.05 dB over DPRNN-Spe, +0.58 dB over SpEx+, ~30k extra params, larger gains under mismatched (voice-memo) references. **Admission-gated**: re-encode only when cosine(enrollment, refined embedding) clears a per-SIR-bin threshold, else fall back to the original enrollment. (Citation hygiene: the +1.05 dB is IRA's; IRA never reported a confusion rate — DCF-Net owns the 0.4%.) **Papers:** Deng et al., Interspeech 2021, arXiv:2011.02102; SpEx++ (frame-level reference, reserve for teacher). **SIR:** 0/−5 dB fit; at −12/−15 dB the gate admits rarely — stratified experiment needed. **Why #9:** hackathon-shaped two-stage with tiny params; directly repairs noisy voice-memo enrollment. **Risk:** ungated refinement latches onto the dominant interferer (Zhao's semantic-latch mechanism); an ungated IRA arm is expected to show elevated NSR. **Difficulty:** easy.

### 10. Positive/negative enrollment comparison

Capture two enrollments — target-present (positive) and target-absent same-environment (negative, ≥0.3 s) — and condition on the *difference* via a Siamese comparison head ("in positive but not negative = target"): +2.1 dB SI-SNRi on noisy-enrollment TSE, causal-friendly, public code. Converts noisy enrollment from liability into disambiguating evidence; the counterweight to self-enrollment poisoning. Fallback: fixed noise clip as negative when only clean target audio exists. **Papers:** Xu et al., NeurIPS 2025, arXiv:2502.16611; CLAPSep negative queries, TASLP 2024, arXiv:2402.17455 (independent validation; gains largest where the positive query is weakest). **SIR:** 0/−5 dB measured; −15 dB untested. **Why #10:** one extra embedding path per sample, ~1 day; directly hardens the identity path. **Risk:** two-capture demo UX cost; positive enrollment still needs target-dominant speech; no streaming/negative-SIR/phone evidence. **Difficulty:** easy.

### 11. Extract-first-dereverb-second (ordering + target discipline)

The post-stage sees one RIR, not two; at −15 dB a pre-dereverb stage trains on interferer-dominated statistics. Three published precedents (Chazan, MUSE, MERL) back the order. For close-mic phone (0.3–1 m): default **direct-path target** (Venkatesh close-mic finding, WHAMR! convention); one sanctioned ablation = direct + 20–30 ms early reflections, judged by gate cosine + listening, never DNSMOS; reject 500 ms windows. Single-channel dereverb primitive: KP-FCP (Kronecker-decomposed forward convolutive prediction, P≈5, the phone-real-time variant) between two enrollment-conditioned DNNs — DNN₁ 6.1 → DNN₁+msFCP+DNN₂ 12.2 dB on SMS-WSJ 1ch. The enrollment-gate↔dereverb interaction is unmeasured anywhere: treat SECS as a first-class dereverb metric per SIR stratum. **Papers:** Chazan et al. 2023, arXiv:2303.07072; MUSE, ASRU 2023, arXiv:2310.08277; Zhu et al., KP-FCP, arXiv:2510.24471 (ICASSP 2026); Venkatesh grid study. **SIR:** 0 dB default; ordering argument strengthens at −15 dB. **Why #11:** zero-cost training-target and stage-order decision; dereverb is a real phone-room constraint. **Risk:** FCP filter estimation becomes interferer-dominated at −15 dB (CxNet FCP-ESSU is the post-demo fix); added algorithmic latency must fit the frame deadline. **Difficulty:** easy–medium.

### 12. Enrollment-conditioned pVAD soft gate (compute saver + negative anchor)

A 80–130K-param 3-class personal VAD (non-speech / target / non-target): (a) compute gate — skip the heavy extractor when the target is silent; (b) confusion supervisor — flag frames where output matches the non-target channel; (c) enrollment-free degraded mode. Use as **soft prior** (3-way posteriors), never hard cuts (hard gating costs ~0.7 dB). Training discipline: 20%-target-absent protocol + enrollment-less fallback; Gaussian smoothing + double-threshold hysteresis from day one. Wearer self-speech as a negative anchor (holder speech is never the target — cheapest poisoning guard for bank updates). **Papers:** Ding et al., arXiv:1908.04284; Personal VAD 2.0, arXiv:2204.03793; Foreground VAD, arXiv:2609.19856 (1–2 ms/frame CPU); USEF-TP, arXiv:2501.03612 (joint TSE+PVAD). **SIR:** 0/−5 dB as compute gate; −15 dB is the dominant failure mode (head latches onto the louder interferer) — stratify head accuracy per SIR bin before trusting it. **Why #12:** massive always-on compute savings; the negative anchor is free. **Risk:** pVAD false negatives cascade into muting the target (tune for recall); hard gating deletes real speech. **Difficulty:** easy.

### 13. Disentangled identity conditioning + nontarget anti-laundering training

Two training-side purifiers, zero inference cost. (a) SDR-TSE: split reference speech into local/semantic, helpful-global (identity), harmful-global (paralinguistics: emotion, rate, prosody) and keep only identity for conditioning, via adaptive-modulation layer norm (AMLN) instead of concatenation — r_scr 6.67 vs 8.56 X-SepFormer. (b) TASE-SVNet nontarget-sample training: conventional TSE only teaches target enhancement, so on nontarget trials the enhancer is unconstrained and biases output toward the enrolled speaker; constrain it (force suppression on nontarget trials) — +15.2% relative on vehicle-spk, robust in heavy overlap. Directly attacks the −15 dB confusion failure (wrong-speaker output when the target is quiet). **Papers:** Mu et al., AAAI 2024, arXiv:2312.10305; Zhang et al., ICASSP 2021, arXiv:2103.08781. **SIR:** 0/−5 dB; −15 dB untested — aggressive purification may strip prosody that is the only identity cue in a short enrollment. **Why #13:** both attack confusion at the training level with no runtime cost. **Risk:** nontarget-mixture construction needs care; AMLN×INT8-ECAPA interaction unmeasured. **Difficulty:** medium.

### 14. Identity-gated pseudo-label admission for real-data adaptation (RemixIT-TSE recipe)

The first published TSE remixing loop: 3-stage progressive adaptation (synthetic pretrain → target-aware weakly-supervised → real-only RemixIT loop) on causal USEF-TFGridNet, with quality-filtered pseudo-label admission (SIM ≥ τ_SIM and TER ≤ τ_TER — only 4.2 h of 51.03 h retained, ~8%) plus target-only supervision and EMA teacher: −6.53% rel TER, +21.84% speaker similarity on REAL-TSE EVAL-2. Our addition, which no published loop has: **per-SIR-bin enrollment-cosine admission on every pseudo-target** — every published loop is identity-blind and assigns pseudo-targets by prominence, which at −15 dB means laundering a cleanly-separated *interferer* as ground truth. Verdict across five survey waves: loops are post-demo adaptation machinery, never demo-critical. **Papers:** Wang, Guan, Wei & Long, arXiv:2609.35118 (code public); PS4, arXiv:2607.08111 (adopt corpus recipe + VAD/ASR proxies; its SIM-ranking *loss* violates the gate-only rule — adopt as admission, not optimisation). **SIR:** 0 dB adaptation proven; −5/−15 dB only with the identity gate. **Why #14:** the only published worked TSE adaptation loop; the ~8% admission rate is the honest calibration of how much pseudo-label a loop can trust. **Risk:** admission rate collapses at −15 dB; quality filters starve exactly the tail we need — gate on identity, never quality. **Difficulty:** medium (repo public; ~1–2 weeks with calibration).

### 15. TFACM cache-memory core + enrollment-initialised identity bank

TFACM-Small (~0.5M): cache memory *re-encoding* (H,C) stops long-stream degradation (+2.3 dB over SkiM on 9 s inputs); on top, a MoMuSE/MeMo-style bank — **enrollment initialises the anchor** (never self-enroll from cold), updates gated by enrollment↔output similarity (never blind FIFO), separate speaker and contextual banks. Zero-cost training auxiliaries: predictive-SkiM/CPC on memory states. Zegers constraint: long memory carries identity only, deep layers; short memory carries phones/formants, shallow layers. Post-demo upgrade: Gated DeltaNet cell (ICLR 2025) — input-dependent decay as emergency poisoning clearance. **Papers:** Chen et al., arXiv:2505.13094; MoMuSE, arXiv:2412.08247; MeMo, arXiv:2507.15294. **SIR:** 0 dB ✓ (as separation); −12 dB and below is the predicted divergence point — per-SIR-bin ablation is an open measurement. **Why #15:** the only memory design that survives the poisoning analysis (MeMo's blind FIFO is the cautionary precedent); streaming-native. **Risk:** memory poisoning at negative SIR (EvoTSE τ=0 ablation: unguarded refinement *worse* than no loop); admission-threshold calibration unmeasured anywhere. **Difficulty:** medium–hard (novel composition).

### 16. Confusion-aware loss stack (all training-only, zero inference cost)

Primary: per-source **mean-of-dB negative SI-SDR** (a −15 dB target gets one full vote instead of ~3% of the loss mass — the SA-SDR energy trap) + **ε-tSDR** floor (SDRmax≈30 dB) for silent/target-absent references. Then: asymmetric deletion penalty **swept per SIR bin** (α=10 was tuned at positive SNR; expect the U-shaped α* curve's minimum to shift at negative SIR — unpublished curve); X-SepFormer chunk-level confusion reweighting (r_scr 8.10 vs 9.51 baseline); SC-TSE centroid consistency loss (λ≈0.1, CLS ω 1.0→0.8); TripleC cross-condition consistency (w=50, easy-SIR outputs anchor hard-SIR outputs). Freeze curriculum + LR before any loss ablation (Kolbæk). Never energy-proportional weighting (wSDR structurally down-weights the target exactly when it's quiet). **Papers:** Le Roux et al., ICASSP 2019, arXiv:1811.02508; von Neumann et al., ICASSP 2022, arXiv:2110.15581; Eskimez et al., ICASSP 2022, arXiv:2110.09625; Liu et al., ICASSP 2023, arXiv:2303.05023; Wu et al., arXiv:2507.09510; TripleC, arXiv:2512.04945. **SIR:** all — regime-agnostic, but the per-bin α* curve at −15 dB is ours to measure. **Why #16:** pure training-side changes stackable on any backbone; directly optimises confusion. **Risk:** chunk-reweighting and asymmetric penalty may double-count the same failure — ablate jointly; optimise for the wrong metric and you get over-suppression. **Difficulty:** easy.

### 17. Seeded attractor speaker state (enrollment-seeded, never stream-discovered)

No published paper builds an attractor decoder *inside* a streaming TSE network — but all pieces are published: seed the attractor from the **enrollment embedding** (AED-EEND: 0.5 s enrollment suffices), never discover from the stream (stream-discovered prominence inverts at −15 dB — the loud interferer wins; Foreground VAD, arXiv:2609.19856, formalises exactly why); update via cheap sum/count EMA with t_low/t_up/t_d admission discipline; gate new-speaker admission with enrollment-similarity, not EDA's existence-probability stopping rule; calibrate admission per SIR bin; explicit rollback when a past admission is judged faulty ("absolute decision corrupts absolutely", ICASSP 2023). Attractor drives a frame-level target-activity/mask prior into the causal separator. **Papers:** AED-EEND lineage; OTS-VAD (8.14% DER AliMeeting); Foreground VAD, arXiv:2609.19856; HyWA, arXiv:2510.12947 (hypernetwork enrollment→VAD weight deltas, zero speech-time overhead — the cheapest enrollment personalization for the always-on gate). **SIR:** 0 dB ✓ (diarization evidence); −15 dB mechanistically the safest admission rule, unmeasured. **Why #17:** seed-from-enrollment is the only admission rule that doesn't invert at −15 dB. **Risk:** burst onset when the interferer starts talking — admission latency vs look-ahead budget is the open question; novel composition, no published whole. **Difficulty:** medium–hard.

### 18. Over-suppression guardrails + conservative fallback stack (loud-target-in-quiet)

The system must handle all SIR regimes, including loud-target-in-quiet: (a) training — asymmetric loss penalising over-suppression (VoiceFilter-Lite pattern; PLCPA-ASYM roughly halved TSOS 14.51%→6.80%), hybrid delta-spectrum continuity loss, TSOS tracked; (b) runtime — overlap/noise-type classification head blending enhanced and raw features per frame (adaptive suppression), plug-and-play bypass so clean signal passes through untouched (worst case = "do nothing"). Evaluate over-suppression and leakage jointly (TSOS + ΔN + DEL/WER); optimising either alone re-creates the trade-off. **Papers:** Eskimez et al., ICASSP 2022 (TSOS metric; E3Net); Taherian et al., ICASSP 2023 (pVAD-gated cross-task KD — frames the auxiliary pVAD misclassifies are excluded from "output zeros" targets); Pan et al., arXiv:2203.16843. **SIR:** purpose-built for +6/+12 dB and target-absent; validates the all-regimes requirement. **Why #18:** the demo fails in the *easy* regime without this — over-suppression of a clean target is the most user-audible failure. **Risk:** asymmetric loss alone worsens leakage; overlap detectors trained at 0–5 dB need ±15 dB validation. **Difficulty:** easy.

### 19. Separate-then-select with tiny steering (the baseline to beat)

Blind separator + counting first, freeze, then train only a small enrollment-conditioned selector — or cheapest of all, post-hoc enrollment verification (cosine/PLDA on output streams, ~0 params; MUSE's freeze-then-train recipe for the trained variant; Plug-and-Steer's C×C linear rerouting at 99.93% routing). Community demos prove the shape ships today. Recommended attack order: post-hoc gate → enrollment-conditioned steering if gating underperforms → unified alignment only if both fail. **But:** keep direct conditioning for the demo path — MUSE's 5-mix collapse (−1.3 dB) is the structural warning that TSE heads inherit separator errors, and at −15 dB any blind separator stage amplifies the loud interferer. **Papers:** MUSE, ASRU 2023, arXiv:2310.08277; Plug-and-Steer, Interspeech 2026, arXiv:2603.19697; SepTDA attractor matching. **SIR:** 0 dB ✓; selection accuracy stratified to −15 dB unmeasured anywhere. **Why #19:** fastest baseline that *must* be beaten (a few lines on any frozen separator); excellent falsification instrument for the direct-conditioning thesis. **Risk:** at −15 dB the separator's streams may both be garbage — routing can't fix a broken separator. **Difficulty:** easy.

### 20. Detect → mask → repair loop (confidence-gated)

Five-rung ladder: (1) identity-free per-frame confidence detector (FCS-style, trained on (α,β)-simulated system outputs); (2) mask the worst ~300 ms unreliable segments; (3) masked-audio-recovery with **frame-level enrollment cross-attention** as context (four independent lines: USEF, SoloSpeech latent fusion +1.8 dB, SEF-VC, RefXVC hybrid Q/K/V); (4) ECAPA cosine τ≈0.5 reliability gate before admitting any repair into an evolving enrollment (τ=0 performs *below* baseline — the memory-poisoning proof); (5) Zhao subtract-recover as last resort. Rung-by-rung: enrollment masking (θ≈30% on the cue) is a one-line training augmentation — do first. **Papers:** C²AV-TSE FCS; SoloSpeech, arXiv:2505.19314; EvoTSE, arXiv:2604.06810 (Zikai Liu — not "Deng et al."); Zhao et al., arXiv:2204.01355. **SIR:** the −15 dB candidate design — mixture-masking trains the repair ability that only matters where local evidence is destroyed. **Why #20:** the only design that *repairs* destroyed local evidence rather than just suppressing; enrollment masking is nearly free. **Risk:** no published audio-only MAR for TSE — the composed loop is our synthesis; two-pass inference needs latency budgeting. **Difficulty:** medium (rung 1 first; full loop hard).

### 21. Staged decomposition / delete-first-insert-second

The DNS-2021-winning pattern: magnitude denoise → magnitude dereverb → complex residual refinement → classical post-processor; coarse 0th-order → high-order refinement (GaGNet); decomposition beats scale even at 560K params matching 4× larger systems. Formalised by Mask2Flow-TSE's D/I law: every generative step is deletion-dominant (~94% step 1) — so spend deletion in a one-pass discriminative masker and keep any generative refiner single-step-from-masked-prior or not at all. Training-recipe corollary: dereverb/denoise front-end (DNN-WPE or FCP, causal, single forward pass) → TSE, pretrain separately, joint-fine-tune; under real-time constraints the cascade can win outright (Neri & Braun: 10× cheaper AND better than SepFormer on REAL-M). **Papers:** SDDNet (Interspeech 2021); GaGNet, arXiv:2106.11789; Mask2Flow-TSE (2026); DESNet (+2 dB joint training). **SIR:** 0 dB ✓ (enhancement evidence); whether staged interferer-suppression→target-recovery helps at −15 dB is our experiment. **Why #21:** stages can be built incrementally — each stage is independently demoable. **Risk:** waveform handoffs between stages propagate errors — use feature/embedding interfaces; audit algorithmic latency per stage (hidden future-frame usage flagged in community reimplementations). **Difficulty:** medium.

### 22. Asymmetric enroll-verify + raw-PCM enrollment discipline + mixed-precision quant

Three deployment disciplines, one approach. (a) **Asymmetric enroll-verify:** enrollment happens once, so use the full float ECAPA-512 there; only the real-time verification/conditioning encoder gets shrunk+quantized (INT8) — 3.07%→2.31% EER with zero added verification cost. (b) **Raw PCM end-to-end** for capture/storage/encoding of enrollment; continuous frozen ECAPA, never quantized timbre tokens (codec identity tax measured three ways: EER 2.21%→3.38% on tokens; ~0.05–0.07 SECS per quantization step; tokens leak voiceprints by construction — SpInv ≥0.70 cosine at 3 s — so codec-latent enrollment is identity-opaque, not identity-preserving). If lossy transport is unavoidable: DAC family at highest affordable bitrate + codec augmentation on the identity path + post-codec threshold recalibration. (c) **Mixed-precision quant budget:** INT8 backbone weights/activations; FP16 conv I/O (INT8 I/O costs ~1.3 dB); ECAPA at INT8 minimum, never 4-bit (ECAPA's sharp weight distribution collapses); interior MLPs mixed INT16/4–8-bit; PQK order (prune+QAT jointly → KD last); uniform INT8 everywhere is a ~3–4 dB tax. **Papers:** Lin et al., arXiv:2110.04438; Li et al., arXiv:2210.17326; TF-MLPNet, arXiv:2508.03047; Codec-SUPERB, arXiv:2402.13053; LLM.int8 (outlier-dimension principle). **SIR:** all regimes (orthogonal). **Why #22:** protects the identity path through deployment; the asymmetry is free (enrollment is one-time). **Risk:** WER-invisible identity loss — a codec'd chain reports healthy WER while silently erasing identity; quantized models lean more on scene cues — re-measure on phone-recorded noisy enrollment. **Difficulty:** easy (PTQ + calibration in an evening).

### 23. SPCL per-stratum curriculum + staged training

Instructor sets additive SIR strata U(−5,+5)→U(−15,+15) with retention of easier data (never drop); student orders **within** each stratum by pilot-run training dynamics (E→A→H, 30/50/20 carve), prioritising the ambiguous/hard boundary band; per-stratum age parameter λ_s pinned so the −15 dB tail is admitted by construction (a global λ starves it forever — the load-bearing warning). Hand-crafted bins kept only as diagnostic overlay. Module-wise: train separator first, freeze, then train selector — never joint-train from scratch (documented instability); stage TSE module only after internal separation is functional. Control arm: static U(−15,+15) coverage (Aurora-2 warns naive train-on-test-distribution can underperform a shaped one). **Papers:** Liu, Miao & Yamagishi, arXiv:2603.04943 (TSE-Datamap, +0.11 dB over hand-crafted, up to 24.5% rel iSDR); Jiang et al., AAAI 2015 (SPCL); Kumar et al., NeurIPS 2010 (SPL); Braun et al., arXiv:1606.06864 (accordion annealing, +42% rel accuracy at low SNR in ASR — adjacent task, set priors at single-digit dB). **SIR:** designed for −15 dB; moderate beats extreme (IsoNet); hard-first is anti-curriculum in TSE (−0.3 to −0.5 dB). **Why #23:** the data-schedule change that makes the −15 dB regime trainable at all. **Risk:** datamap pilot grid was positive-SNR only; at −15 dB "hard" may mean discriminative-cue starvation, and the ambiguous band may be confusion oscillation — cross-check with NSR/r_scr per stratum. **Difficulty:** easy–medium (one pilot run with per-sample logging, then dataloader scheduling).

### 24. Test-time enrollment augmentation (keyframe averaging)

One evening, no retraining: average noise/RIR-augmented copies of the enrollment into the embedding; or mine the most-enrollment-similar 1 s keyframe from the mixture and fuse additively (Feng et al., matches full-length enrollment after ~5 iterations — the disciplined test-time augmentation). **Strictly behind the identity gate** at −15 dB — the keyframe mine risks latching onto the interferer (EvoTSE's poisoning analysis is the admission template). **Papers:** Feng et al., ICASSP 2026, arXiv:2601.12769; HyWA, arXiv:2510.12947 (hypernetwork enrollment→VAD deltas, zero speech-time overhead). **SIR:** 0/−5 dB safe; −15 dB gated-only. **Why #24:** nearly free robustness for short/noisy voice memos. **Risk:** ungated self-enrollment is momentum toward whoever the model currently hears. **Difficulty:** easy.

### 25. AAT/A-DA enrollment hardening (adversarial augmentation-invariance)

Add a small adversarial augmentation-invariance term (L = L_AAM + λ·L_BCE with gradient reversal against an augmentation-type classifier, λ≈0.01 start, grid {0.001, 0.01, 0.03}) so embeddings become indistinguishable across augmentation types — attacks the session/device confound (T-307: one memo per speaker means session==speaker) without session labels. Verify with probe-classifier discipline: session probe → chance while speaker accuracy holds (Peri/Odyssey-2020 discipline); never trust EER alone. **Do NOT run supervised session-adversarial training on the confounded memos** — removing session info removes speaker info. **Papers:** Zhou et al., arXiv:2402.02699 (A-DA); Huh et al., arXiv:2007.12085 (AAT); Peri et al., ICASSP 2020. **SIR:** enrollment-side; improves gate reliability across all SIRs. **Why #25:** one loss term that directly hardens the identity path against the project's known session confound. **Risk:** GRL oscillation on the tiny 212-memo dataset (CLUB-MI fallback exists); VoxCeleb-scale effect sizes won't transfer — run multiple seeds. **Difficulty:** easy–medium.

### 26. Meta-learned fast weights (AFW) — learnable embedding updater

41k-param associative-memory updater (delta-rule write + forgetting) meta-trained closed-loop (backprop through 13–26 s scenarios, updater exposed to its own contaminated evidence). Beats the best oracle-activity-gated heuristic by +3.0 dB under severe enrollment–mixture mismatch, cuts confusion 31%→4%, <5% runtime overhead (rank-1 write + matvecs, no autograd). Tested at TMR −10/−15 dB (holds, doesn't over-adapt). Frames every heuristic as a stability–plasticity frontier that even perfect gating can't escape. **Papers:** Ma, He & Mesgarani, arXiv:2609.20463 (Sep 2026). **SIR:** 0/−5 dB core target; −10/−15 dB tested for the updater itself. **Why #26:** the only learned updater with −10/−15 dB evidence and tiny runtime cost; post-demo identity-bank updater with a name. **Risk:** meta-training needs long single-speaker sessions; untested on phone acoustics; needs the closed-loop protocol replicated on our data. **Difficulty:** hard (weeks).

### 27. Dual-model streaming cadence (fast extractor + slow identity refresh)

12.5 ms causal extractor stays state-light; a slow model (~1 Hz) refreshes long-range conversational/identity embeddings — avoids attention's quadratic growth with session length. Self-VAD as a cheap negative anchor (top-mic energy: anything matching self-voice is not the target) and room-calibration signal. Cheap analog: causal extractor fed by periodically refreshed enrollment/gate embedding. **Papers:** Hu et al., EMNLP 2025, arXiv:2511.11473; EgoSpeak, arXiv:2502.14892; Veluri et al., CHI 2024, arXiv:2405.06289 (7.01 dB, 8 ms in 6.24 ms embedded CPU — the phone-feasibility existence proof). **SIR:** engineering pattern, regime-agnostic. **Why #27:** the streaming architecture wrapper that makes long sessions affordable. **Risk:** self/turn-taking anchors answer "who is engaged?", not "whose voice is this?" — a complement to enrollment conditioning, never a replacement; the partner at −15 dB is unproven. **Difficulty:** medium.

### 28. LExt streaming-safe prompt concatenation

Prepend the enrollment waveform to the mixture as an onset prompt — zero parameters, zero modules (24.1 dB SI-SDR WSJ0-2mix under TFGridNetV2). Folded prompt (LexTra) halves prepended length via P=2 pseudo-channels at ~65% FLOPs/~73% RTF with no accuracy loss; SR-MHA adds parameter-free split-role heads (speaker-aware vs context-aware) for clean-enrollment/noisy-mixture mismatch. Streaming-safe transform: precompute enrollment into a stored conditioning form (folded/subsampled prompt bank, frame embeddings) and feed as side info to a causal backbone — never prepend at inference time. The zero-parameter baseline pole: any learned adapter must beat waveform-concatenation to earn its parameters. **Papers:** LExt, arXiv:2505.05114 (IEEE TASLP); LexTra, arXiv:2601.13948; MC-LExt. **SIR:** 0 dB ✓ (offline); −15 dB is the primary untested risk — the onset cue may latch onto the loud interferer. **Why #28:** the cheapest possible conditioning; sets the bar every fancier interface must clear. **Risk:** non-causal by construction — the streaming-safe variant is our composition, unpublished; artificial target-onset is a capture hazard. **Difficulty:** easy (offline); medium (streaming-safe variant).

### 29. Learnable prompt slot with ECAPA substitution (TUSS/FasTUSS pattern)

A unified separation backbone carries learnable prompt vectors in dedicated slots; substitute ECAPA-derived enrollment vectors (+ APT-style adapter for the classifier→extractor embedding mismatch) into the unclaimed per-speaker prompt slot (TUSS names it as future work). INDPROMPT discipline: enrollment precomputed-and-cached, never looks at the mixture; prompt→loss pairing trained (e.g. identity-gated asymmetric loss under the `<ex-NegSIR>` prompt — Mitsui's published mechanism), never bolted on. Prompt pool variant: diarized slots with confusion-gated selection. **Papers:** TUSS, ICASSP 2025, arXiv:2410.23987; FasTUSS, WASPAA 2025, arXiv:2507.11435; APT, arXiv:2311.18399 (prompt-only tuning fixes embedding mismatch); Mitsui et al., IWAENC 2026, arXiv:2609.11092. **SIR:** 0 dB plausible; −15 dB unknown. **Why #29:** reuses a unified backbone for the speaker-extraction task with minimal surgery. **Risk:** genuine unpublished experiment; selection among pool slots must be identity-gated, never prominence-based; causal tax + enrollment noise may interact badly. **Difficulty:** medium–hard.

### 30. Auxiliary-cue fallbacks (text/style/dialogue, non-speech pre-filter)

When enrollment is absent/contaminated, switchable secondary cues trained jointly with enrollment (CSE recipe — any single cue suffices at inference): dialogue-history text cue (>90% target-stream ID from two turns — strongest published enrollment-free cue); StyleTSE natural-language style prompt (text-only 16.41 dB; stronger on accent/pitch/gender, weaker on identity — secondary disambiguator, identity stays primary); TCE turn-taking dynamics as orthogonal resolver. Text cues don't degrade with SIR, making them the graceful-degradation rung at −15 dB. Non-speech interferers only (TV, music): SoundBeam-style class-label TSE as a pre-filter — dodges the enrollment-asymmetry problem since classes are stable. **Papers:** StyleTSE, ICASSP 2025, arXiv:2501.09169 (+ TextrolMix corpus); CSE (dialogue history); TCE, Interspeech 2024, arXiv:2407.11277; SoundBeam. **SIR:** contextual/diagnostic — graceful degradation at −15 dB. **Why #30:** the only rung that doesn't degrade with SIR; cheap insurance for enrollment failure. **Risk:** text is a weak identity carrier; every fallback rung must sit behind the identity gate; cue-switchable joint training is slow. **Difficulty:** medium (fallbacks); slow (joint training).

### 31. No-enrollment fallback: foreground VAD as gating signal

Frame-synchronous detection of only the *dominant* speaker from supervision (foreground-only labels + competing-speaker mixing augmentation) — 1–2 ms/frame on CPU, beats commercial VADs and enrollment-based systems on foreground selectivity. Enrollment tells you *who*; activity tells you *when*; combining attacks confusion from two sides. **Hard limit:** at −15 dB the dominant speaker *is* the interferer, so any enrollment-free foreground state locks onto the wrong speaker by construction — this is the prominence-inversion anti-pattern, formalised. Use as a gate/fallback only, never as the selector; never let it override the enrollment gate. **Papers:** Yang et al., arXiv:2609.19856 (2026). **SIR:** 0 dB ✓; −15 dB is the anti-pattern — documented, not a gap. **Why #31:** tiny, fast, and honest about its limits; useful degraded mode. **Risk:** selecting the dominant speaker at −15 dB is exactly wrong. **Difficulty:** easy.

### 32. S4D streaming core (default) / selective-Mamba challenger

Fixed-size SSM recurrence as the causal separator core. **Default: S4D** — cheap real-only diagonal recurrence, ONNX-exportable, no distillation pipeline; the Slytherin caveat strengthens the default (ConMamba is not significantly more efficient than Conformer until 30 s memory / 60 s speed — for short phone utterances there is no a priori runtime reason to prefer selective Mamba). **Challenger: selective Mamba** — SepMamba causal U-Net; DPMamba dual-path causal (cite v2 / ICASSP 2025, arXiv:2403.18257 — v1 had a train/eval code error); causal Uni-Mamba with conv front end; conditioning via FiLM-before-SSM (default; three lines agree) or CrossMamba clue-as-query (cheap challenger, causal variant exists); SA-Mamba in-recurrence stays one experimental arm gated on a −12 dB win. Decision rule: −12 dB SI-SDRi + NSR/r_scr, multiple seeds, within-stratum CIs, measured single-thread phone-class RTF with deadline histogram — never MACs, never aggregate, never single-seed. **Papers:** SpeakerBeam-SS, Interspeech 2024 (−78% RTF vs causal Conv-TasNet); CrossMamba, arXiv:2409.04803 (ICASSP 2025); RT-SEMamba, Interspeech 2026 (progressive 8→1 KD, 2.75× speedup, 25 ms latency — the canonical distillation template). **SIR:** 0 dB measured; −15 dB unmeasured. **Why #32:** the streaming-core decision the whole architecture rests on; S4D is the low-risk default. **Risk:** selective Mamba's input-dependent projections complicate quantisation/scheduling (non-selective S4D deploys cleanly); no phone-CPU RTF for any Mamba candidate anywhere. **Difficulty:** easy (S4D default); medium–hard (Mamba challenger with KD pipeline).

### 33. SkiM causal skip-memory separator (default skeleton)

Dual-path RNN where a segment-LSTM skims frame states and a memory-LSTM skips updates on redundant frames — 75% less compute than DPRNN at 17.1 dB SDRi, sub-ms algorithmic latency, RTF 0.46 on weak hardware. Swap Seg-LSTM→Seg-SRU (SRU > GRU > LSTM in Swift-Net's causal ablation); add Predictive-SkiM's CPC future-memory prediction as zero-inference-cost training. Separator is blind — enrollment bolts on as selector/conditioning. **Papers:** SkiM, arXiv:2201.10800 (Interspeech 2022); SRU, arXiv:1709.02755; Swift-Net, ECAI 2025. **SIR:** 0 dB measured; −5 dB plausible; −15 dB unmeasured. **Why #33:** the only causal separator simultaneously causal, measured-real-time on weak CPU, and quality-competitive — the pragmatic skeleton. **Risk:** LSTM cell-state drift on unbounded always-on streams; Mem-LSTM history needs windowing. **Difficulty:** easy (community streaming recipes exist).

### 34. Waveformer hybrid (causal DCC encoder + one shallow transformer decoder)

Dilated causal-conv encoder with cacheable states pays O(log R) vs attention's O(R) per chunk; a single masked transformer decoder layer adds generalisation without the full self-attention tax; enrollment embedding enters as the decoder query (FiLM/cross-attention). Measured 9.43 dB SI-SNRi, RTF 0.66–0.94 on desktop i5 — the documented best RTF-per-dB hybrid, 2–3 dB over causal baselines. **Papers:** Waveformer, arXiv:2211.02250 (ICASSP 2023); LLVC (20 ms CPU voice conversion). **SIR:** 0 dB measured; no Waveformer-based enrollment-conditioned TSE at negative SIR exists. **Why #34:** best documented RTF-per-dB hybrid; decoder is one layer. **Risk:** desktop-i5 RTF doesn't transfer to phone (43–70% of Snapdragon transformer latency is layout transforms); decoder self-attention pays the mobile plumbing tax. **Difficulty:** easy–medium.

### 35. TDANet enrollment-anchored top-down signal

TDANet's global-attention (GA) module produces one global signal modulating every encoder scale — a single choke point where the mixture-derived statistic is replaced/fused with an enrollment vector, cheaper than FiLM-at-every-block or frame-level cross-attention (identity enters as channel-mixing modulation: FFN-only 12.5 > MHSA-only 11.9). Already phone-budget: 2.3M params, CPU RTF 0.79, ~10% of Sepformer MACs at ~91% quality. TDFNet proves the junction accepts a foreign conditioning stream. Causalise the GA via running/cumulative pooling; refresh the stale top-down signal on N-cycles **gated by enrollment↔output cosine** (MC-LExt: downsampled static enrollment, ~37% compute cut). **Papers:** TDANet (Pandey et al., ICASSP); TDFNet; LexTra/MC-LExt (2026 cost analogues). **SIR:** 0 dB measured (−5..+5 dB LRS2); −15 dB unmeasured — mixture-derived GA is interferer-dominated there, and whether the enrollment anchor flips that is unmeasured. **Why #35:** a single choke point for conditioning is the cheapest architectural place to put identity. **Risk:** no streaming TDANet exists; the N-schedule × gate experiment is ours to run. **Difficulty:** medium–hard.

### 36. SuDoRM-RF + GC3 + FiLM TSE (compression-first pure-conv)

Cheapest pure-conv time-domain separator (17 dB WSJ0-2mix at 45× DPRNN FLOPs for the 0.25x variant; causal C-SuDoRM-RF++ runs ~10–20× real-time on laptop CPU at 8 kHz). FiLM-before-each-U-ConvBlock is the established conditioning interface. GC3 is the proven plug-and-play compressor on this exact host (2.5% size / 17.6% complexity, sometimes better quality via receptive-field stretch). **Papers:** SuDoRM-RF, arXiv:2103.02644; C-SuDoRM-RF++; GC3. **SIR:** 0 dB measured; −15 dB unmeasured; global-context limitation at extreme SIR untested. **Why #36:** cleanest scaling curve (0.79M → 13.39 dB); compression proven on the host. **Risk:** no phone-CPU RTF (laptop-only timings); GC3 numbers are blind-separation, interaction with enrollment conditioning unmeasured. **Difficulty:** easy.

### 37. Attention-free / separable-attention phone economics

Two tiers. (a) **No attention at all:** TF-MLPNet (0.509M, 12.37 dB SISDR, streaming, quantized with 0.6 dB loss, real-time 6 ms chunks on a GAP9 hearable chip weaker than any phone, TSE-capable with FiLM) and TIGER (0.82M, 18.0 dB LibriMix, interleaved TF modelling). (b) If attention is kept, separable/linear forms only: MobileViTv2 O(k) element-wise separable attention (3.2× faster than MHA on mobile), MobiAtt O(ND) linear attention (~20% CoreML saving). Ranking: no attention → single shallow chunk-bounded layer → separable → linear → full MHA (only with on-phone measurement). Core finding: attention is memory-bound, not compute-bound, on phones. **Papers:** TF-MLPNet, arXiv:2508.03047; TIGER (ICLR 2025); MobileViTv2, arXiv:2206.02680; SmartMem, arXiv:2404.13528. **SIR:** 0 dB measured; unmeasured at negative SIR. **Why #37:** the compute discipline that keeps the demo inside the phone budget. **Risk:** TF-MLPNet's numbers are denoising + hearable silicon, not competing-talker TSE on phone CPU; vision→audio transfer of separable attention is speculative. **Difficulty:** easy.

### 38. MERL close-talk→far-field pseudo-target pipeline

Project each close-talk (headset/lapel) mic to the far-field phone mic via an estimated **causal 10 ms Wiener filter** (160 taps) → synthetic clean pseudo-targets for real mixtures; then a 4-stage easy→hard curriculum (fully-overlapped → noisy conversations → simulated far-field → real far-field with pseudo-targets) plus a data-hygiene chain: enhancement → speaker-cosine ≥0.85 filter → WER filter → forced alignment → 5% non-target zero-output samples. Baseline TER 70% → 37%. **Uniquely survives −15 dB**: supervision comes from the close-talk mic where the target still dominates. **Papers:** MERL winner analysis; Nakatani et al., ICASSP 2026 ("close-to-distant microphone projection"); REAL-TSE overview, arXiv:2607.15198. **SIR:** engineered for 0/−5 dB; −15 dB survives via close-talk supervision. **Why #38:** the only real-data pipeline whose supervision doesn't collapse at −15 dB. **Risk:** close-talk cross-talk contamination at high overlap; sync/calibration of independent phone clocks; ~$20 lapel mic per participant + collection effort — data cost, not code cost. **Difficulty:** medium (days once recordings exist; collection is the work).

### 39. Dynamic-SIR synthesis + SIR-histogram-matched pipeline

(a) Per-sub-utterance gain scheduling from BS.1770-gated loudness, reverb applied *after* level-setting via on-the-fly FRAM-RIR (one RIR per speaker per mixture, CPU, ~110× ISM speed); (b) motion-driven variant with time-varying RIRs along speaker trajectories (SonicSim: ~360 h speech over 57k trajectories, ICLR 2025, CUDA docker public). Evaluation discipline: **never report single-utterance SI-SDRi on a dynamic file** — stratify per-second SI-SDRi/NSR/r_scr by logged local SIR. Before any curriculum, **audit the SIR histogram of real recordings** (real data is inevitably SIR-imbalanced; at −15 dB our whole regime is the underrepresented band). EARS (anechoic, whisper-to-yell dynamic range) gives *natural* −15 dB cases rather than gain-faked ones — keep natural-effort and gain-faked as separate evaluation strata. **Papers:** LibriMix, arXiv:2005.11262; SonicSim, ICLR 2025, arXiv:2410.01481; Li & Seki, Interspeech 2024, arXiv:2406.13982 (SNR-imbalance remixing + curriculum); EARS, arXiv:2406.06185. **SIR:** the recipe that makes −15 dB training and eval designable at all. **Why #39:** without this, every −15 dB experiment is measuring gain-faking artefacts. **Risk:** gain-scheduled mixtures miss motion cues; motion-driven synthesis has unvalidated phone toolchains. **Difficulty:** easy (a); medium (b).

### 40. Full distillation stack (architecture-first → KD → unlabeled → enrollment-side → PQK)

Rung order, each independently verifiable: (1) train the largest affordable *offline* teacher on U(−15,+15) SIR with asymmetric enrollment; (2) layer-wise KD + objective shifting (distill teacher intermediates into a streamable student, anneal teacher-following → oracle-label so the student escapes the teacher's ceiling); (3) unlabeled-data KD on real phone recordings, guarded by ASR or enrollment-similarity MTL; (4) enrollment-side KD: Serre's contrastive KD trains a 150k-param tiny speaker encoder from complex embeddings with on-the-fly inference refinement (ICASSP 2025, arXiv:2601.16235 — the "100×" claim is **unverified across two survey waves, do not cite**); (5) PQK last: prune + QAT first, then distill from the full-net teacher. Progressive variant: RT-SEMamba 8→1 dual output+intermediate distillation (≤25 ms latency, ~2.75× speedup) — apply *direct* non-causal→causal, not staged (StreamCodec2's finding). **Papers:** Chen 2021 (layer-wise KD); Thakker/E3Net, ICASSP 2022; Serre et al., arXiv:2601.16235; PQK, Interspeech 2021; RT-SEMamba, Interspeech 2026. **SIR:** 0 dB measured; −15 dB unmeasured — no paper evaluates a distilled student at negative SIR with enrollment conditioning. **Why #40:** the compression path that gets a strong model into the phone budget. **Risk:** teachers trained on symmetric SIR launder their confusion failures into the student without a gated loss; PQK validated on keyword spotting, not separation. **Difficulty:** hard (weeks).

### 41. Conditioning-interaction KD (unclaimed novel rung)

Distill the teacher's *conditioning interaction* itself, not just its outputs: prompt-vector matching (TUSS-style cross-prompt targets), disentangled-target matching (SDR-TSE identity stream), mixture-reproduced teacher embeddings (student enrollment encoder reproduces clean-utterance embeddings from the noisy mixture), iterative-inference distillation (TASE-SVNet). DCKD (NeurIPS 2025) is the load-bearing constraint: regulate the privileged channel (MI disentanglement, coarse-to-fine layer placement) *before* distillation, or the student imitates an oracle that never exists at inference. **Enrollment-similarity-gated KD loss** (down-weight teacher pseudo-targets where teacher output disagrees with enrollment — CA-MKD's confidence weighting ported to unlabeled TSE data) is the zero-inference-cost novel rung. **Papers:** DCKD (NeurIPS 2025); CA-MKD, arXiv:2201.00007; TASE-SVNet, arXiv:2103.08781. **SIR:** 0 dB by mechanism; −15 dB unmeasured. **Why #41:** unpublished for TSE — a genuine research contribution if it works; zero inference cost. **Risk:** entirely our experiment; teachers' confusion failures need the gate. **Difficulty:** hard.

### 42. UniInterNet-style train-big/deploy-small (non-causal interferer branch dropped at inference)

Train the causal enrollment-conditioned target branch alongside a *non-causal* interferer/undesired branch with unidirectional target→interferer feature interaction at encoder/recurrent/decoder stages; the interferer branch assists learning via backprop, then is dropped at deploy (~75% inference-complexity reduction). Decoder-stage interaction is the most important per ablations. **Papers:** UniInterNet (MDPI Appl. Sci.). **SIR:** all regimes in principle; untested at −12/−15 dB with enrollment conditioning. **Why #42:** interferer modelling without inference cost — composes with the interferer-first thesis. **Risk:** requires retraining with the auxiliary branch; the interferer branch's supervision (negative enrollment vs learned estimate) is an open design choice. **Difficulty:** hard (retraining).

### 43. Soft/permutation-free assignment (Prob-PIT / OR-PIT)

Replace hard min-permutation with soft-minimum/probabilistic permutation treated as a latent variable — at negative SIR the correct permutation's loss is rarely the clear minimum, so hard PIT locks in the wrong assignment exactly when it matters. OR-PIT recurses one-speaker-at-a-time, enabling interferer-first extraction. **Papers:** Yousefi & Hansen, Interspeech 2019 / TASLP 2021 (+1–1.5 dB precedent); Tachibana, ICASSP 2021 (SinkPIT); Kinoshita et al., arXiv:1904.03065 (OR-PIT). **SIR:** −5/−15 dB (assignment-ambiguity regimes); neutral at 0 dB. **Why #43:** one-line loss change that directly attacks wrong-assignment at negative SIR. **Risk:** soft assignment dilutes the gradient where hard PIT is already correct; gains never measured at −15 dB. **Difficulty:** easy (soft-min); medium (OR-PIT recursion).

### 44. Asymmetric deletion penalty, swept per SIR bin

Weight target-energy deletion (estimate weaker than reference) α>1 while under-suppression stays unweighted — "harmless-first" bias toward leaving energy in rather than erasing it. Dual-asymmetric variants pair complex- and magnitude-domain terms under one λ; the λ-sweep prior is U-shaped (cliffs at both extremes, flat middle). **α must be re-swept per SIR bin** — α=10 was tuned at positive SNR; expect α* to shift smaller at negative SIR (unpublished curve). **Papers:** VoiceFilter-Lite, arXiv:2009.04323 (α=10); PLCPA-ASYM, ICASSP 2022, arXiv:2110.09625 (TSOS 14.51%→6.80%); Carson & Dandekar, Interspeech 2026 (U-shaped λ prior). **SIR:** 0 dB (loud-target over-suppression) and −5/−15 dB (quiet-target deletion). **Why #44:** one loss term covering both failure directions. **Risk:** α tuned at positive SNR deletes the −15 dB target anyway; leakage explodes at the other cliff — the sweep is mandatory, not optional. **Difficulty:** easy.

### 45. X-SepFormer chunk-level confusion reweighting

Compute chunk-level SI-SDRi and the confusion rate r_scr; scale or weight the utterance loss by the chunkwise confusion distribution so confused chunks dominate the gradient instead of being mean-averaged away (weighted 8.10 vs scaled 9.03 vs baseline 9.51 r_scr). **Papers:** Liu et al., ICASSP 2023, arXiv:2303.05023. **SIR:** 0/−5 dB (confusion regimes); −15 dB ordering unverified — the chunkwise separability assumption degrades there. **Why #45:** backbone-agnostic, zero inference cost, directly optimises the confusion metric. **Risk:** at −15 dB the SI-SDRi chunk distribution may be noise, making the weights reweight garbage. **Difficulty:** easy (chunk-stat bookkeeping).

### 46. Reference-free runtime confidence estimation (lab + phone diagnostic)

Parameter-free CSE as the on-device "did separation happen" check (one dot product); offline, a blind SI-SNR estimator (REAL-M's ~300K-param recipe, validated by user study against human judgments); ReFESS-QI (PCC 0.95 SI-SNR estimate); NORESQA/SQUIM with our own clean enrollment clips as the non-matching-reference pool; SCOREQ-natural against an averaged 10–20 NMR pool (enrollment excluded from the default pool pending calibration). **None detects wrong-speaker extraction — pair all with the identity gate.** Calibrate every estimator on 50–100 human-rated clips of our own data (out-of-domain collapse is measured: DNSMOS 0.678→0.507). **Papers:** REAL-M, arXiv:2110.10812 (the 20→8→2.8 dB clean-to-real cliff); ReFESS-QI, arXiv:2510.21014; NORESQA, arXiv:2109.08125; SQUIM, arXiv:2304.01448. **SIR:** diagnostics for all regimes. **Why #46:** the diagnostic layer that makes real-recording evaluation honest. **Risk:** SQUIM's MOS output floors near 2.0 and "fails significantly for real phone calls" — suspect exactly in our −15 dB regime. **Difficulty:** easy (hours).

### 47. Real-recording validation protocol

Calibrate everything on real phone audio: REAL-M dataset (1,400+ real two-speaker mixtures) + its blind estimator recipe as the template; REAL-TSE challenge protocol (SpkSim + F1 target-activity + P.808 — target-absent branch: suppress, never hallucinate); Xu's 5000-sample target-confusion protocol (enrollment containing both target and a shared interferer; confused when extracted audio is closer to the interferer) as the wrong-speaker sanity check; ESTOI over STOI/PESQ for intelligibility at low SNR. Median + confusion rate, never mean alone. **Papers:** REAL-M; REAL-TSE; Xu et al., arXiv:2502.16611; Jensen & Taal ESTOI (TASLP 2016). **SIR:** protocol for all; −15 dB calibration is our experiment. **Why #47:** simulated numbers don't transfer (the clean-to-real cliff is ~17 dB); this is the discipline that keeps the demo honest. **Risk:** collecting matched real recordings at −15 dB SIR with ground truth requires careful mixing discipline. **Difficulty:** easy (protocol); medium (data collection).

### 48. Causalization recipe + causal-norm discipline + frame-rate budgeting

The transferable rules, not naive unidirectional swap: causal padding, unidirectional RNNs, triangular causal masks in *every* self-attention (intra- and cross-chunk), left-padded convolutions, streaming state caching (K/V + conv states) for constant per-frame cost, latency accounting (chunk length = floor; per-layer lookahead multiplies by depth). Norm discipline: batch norm is fully causal at inference *iff* eval mode with frozen running statistics — then it fuses into a multiply-add (the lowest-RTF option); layer norm costs ~10% of Conformer inference time for 0.1% of FLOPs (memory-bound) — for TCN stacks use cumulative layer norm; small models may drop norm entirely. Budget: frame rate, not parameter count, dominates streaming CPU cost — size the causal core at ≤1 GFLOP/s for ≥2× phone headroom. **Papers:** Swift-Net, ECAI 2025; RE-SepFormer, arXiv:2206.09507 (11× MAC cut, causal tax ~4.4 dB); Tiny-SepFormer, arXiv:2206.13689; FastEnhancer, arXiv:2509.21867; FusionFormer, arXiv:2210.17079. **SIR:** all (orthogonal); norm choices' effect at −15 dB untested. **Why #48:** the engineering checklist that prevents silent causality and latency bugs. **Risk:** naive causalization of big models degrades substantially; audit every norm placement against the streaming causality checklist. **Difficulty:** easy (checklist applies in an afternoon).

### 49. k-NN voice-conversion synthetic interferer diversity

Generate target-adjacent synthetic interferers (WavLM latent + k-NN regression + HiFi-GAN) as final-stage curriculum material — interferer *diversity*, not count, is the bottleneck; mere extra real interferers did not help. Gains read out on confusion metrics, not mean SI-SDRi. **Papers:** Yun Liu et al., SLT 2024, arXiv:2410.00811. **SIR:** −5 → −15 dB same-speaker-like confusion tails. **Why #49:** the only surveyed method that manufactures the *hard* confusion tail on demand. **Risk:** VC fingerprints are learnable artefacts — strict quarantine from eval; moderate-heavy build (VC pipeline), training-only. **Difficulty:** hard.

### 50. Hybrid multi-resolution delta-spectrum continuity regularizer

Multi-resolution STFT loss on static/delta/acceleration spectral features alongside time-domain SI-SDR; penalises temporally smeared, over-suppressed estimates that SI-SDR alone tolerates (multi-resolution beats any single resolution; delta features load-bearing; −8.6% over-suppression MAE). **Papers:** Wu et al., Interspeech 2022, arXiv:2203.16843 (WER gains with flat SI-SDR); Pan et al. **SIR:** 0/−5 dB (published evidence is WER-only; no NSR/r_scr evidence). **Why #50:** attacks the temporal-smearing failure SI-SDR can't see. **Risk:** pure training-cost increase for downstream-only gains; unknown effect on confusion metrics — time GPU throughput first. **Difficulty:** easy–medium.

### 51. pVAD-gated inactive-target handling + duration weighting

Frames/utterances where the target is absent get VAD-gated handling (exclude from the teacher's "generate zero" target, soft or hard) instead of forcing aggressive suppression; joint personal-VAD head tells the model *when* to extract; duration-weighted SI-SNR tells the loss *what* to care about (+4.17 dB sparse-clean). **Papers:** Taherian et al., ICASSP 2023; Lin et al., arXiv:2106.14371. **SIR:** 0/−5 dB (target-sparse overlap; ambiguous frames are the norm at negative SIR). **Why #51:** stops the model learning to hallucinate-or-mute on target-absent frames. **Risk:** needs a reliable pVAD at −15 dB SIR; excluding ambiguous frames removes supervision exactly where the model is weakest. **Difficulty:** easy–medium.

### 52. ESSER-style noise-discounting for noisy-target training

When training targets are noisy (real phone data), add an auxiliary noise estimate and a λ-weighted noise-discount term plus orthogonality constraint so the loss doesn't demand reconstruction of inseparable noise components (the Maciejewski trap: noisy-oracle SI-SDR collapses to −9 dB at −5 dB training SNR vs +3.5 dB clean-oracle). Cheaper hedge: sample-median loss (median over batch before T-F averaging) — but only valid when the "noise" is sporadic, not a dominant coherent talker. **Papers:** Maciejewski et al., ICASSP 2021, arXiv:2010.12430 (~8 dB gulf at 0 dB training SNR); Saito et al., arXiv:2105.12315. **SIR:** −5 dB noisy-target training; irrelevant at clean-synthetic 0 dB. **Why #52:** unblocks training on real noisy targets. **Risk:** ESSER λ tuning fragile (validation tuning failed at −5 dB); its noise estimate captured out-of-band components only — not a real noise model. **Difficulty:** medium.

### 53. Target-prioritised aggregation weighting (inverse-energy) — open ablation

Deliberately up-weight the quiet target channel in loss aggregation (inverse-energy weighting on wSDR-style terms) instead of accepting energy dilution — the opposite error from the SA-SDR trap. Must be paired with the ε-tSDR floor (amplifies silence otherwise). Zero published evidence at negative SIR; judge on NSR/r_scr, never mean SI-SDR. **Papers:** von Neumann et al., arXiv:2110.15581 (documents the energy trap to invert). **SIR:** −5/−15 dB. **Why #53:** one-line change attacking the exact mechanism that deletes quiet targets. **Risk:** unmeasured; amplifies near-silent references. **Difficulty:** easy.

### 54. Recipe-level over-suppression mitigation (KD + multi-task ASR)

Knowledge distillation to a student on unlabeled data plus multi-task ASR training cut TSOS 14.61→0.60 with the *same loss family* — the strongest published evidence that recipe can dominate loss choice for the deletion axis (the conditional-Kolbæk reading: single-term swaps lose to recipe). Stage before any joint training: TSE module trained only after internal separation is functional. **Papers:** Eskimez et al., "Fast Realtime PSE", arXiv:2204.00771; Eskimez et al., ICASSP 2022, arXiv:2110.09625. **SIR:** 0 dB (loud-target TSOS) and −5 dB. **Why #54:** the deletion-axis evidence that training recipe beats loss choice. **Risk:** MT slightly degrades perceptual quality and raises insertions on long audio; PSE setting, not TSE at negative SIR; heavy-ish build. **Difficulty:** medium–hard.

### 55. Asymmetric enrollment effort (rich enrollment pipeline, lean runtime)

Spend compute at enrollment time — the one-time capture — and keep the streaming path lean: VAD → denoise enrollment → ECAPA embed → segment-attentive aggregation if short → optional positive/negative split or TTS re-synthesis repair (EoW CR/EC via IndexTTS2/xTTS/CosyVoice3 from a wake-word prompt — post-demo). Synthetic enrollment admits only via the identity battery: SECS ≥ threshold, ARI cluster-structure ranking, WER-neutrality — **never** on DNSMOS (DNSMOS and WER diverge under TTS augmentation: CosyVoice3 DNSMOS 2.107 vs WER 102.54%). Capture-side: three-stage pipeline (energy gate → MOS gate → speaker-consistency gate, cosine-restart T≈0.87). **Papers:** Lin et al. enrollment-side effort; EoW, Yang et al. 2026; Liu, Interspeech 2024; DECAF (identity loss can be WER-invisible — why the SECS battery is mandatory); ZeSTA, arXiv:2603.04219 (naive TTS augmentation *degrades* speaker similarity ~6.5% relative — park TTS augmentation for the hackathon). **SIR:** improves the enrollment cue at all regimes; doesn't fix −15 dB mixture dominance by itself. **Why #55:** the enrollment is the one place where spending compute is free at runtime. **Risk:** TTS fingerprints + identity drift; thresholds untested across devices/languages. **Difficulty:** easy–medium (pipeline glue; TTS repair post-demo).

### 56. Enrollment-side distillation (tiny speaker encoder)

Serre's contrastive KD trains a 150k-param tiny speaker encoder from complex embeddings, then runs *inside* the PSE system at inference for on-the-fly enrollment refinement (ICASSP 2025, arXiv:2601.16235). The concrete precedent for tiny-refiner + frozen-ECAPA anchor; refinement must be similarity-gated per frame. **The "100×" compression claim is unverified across two survey waves — do not cite it.** **SIR:** 0 dB; −15 dB unmeasured. **Why #56:** shrinks the identity path that otherwise dominates the runtime budget. **Risk:** unverified headline claim; on-the-fly refinement needs the gate. **Difficulty:** medium.

### 57–60. Post-demo only (ranked among themselves)

**57. Codec-latent streaming masker.** Frozen streamable codec encoder (FocalCodec-Stream, ICASSP 2026, arXiv:2509.16195 — causal, 80 ms latency, open weights) → FiLM masker with ECAPA in the prompt slot → codec decoder. Evidence: CodecSep, TMLR 2026, arXiv:2509.11717 (1.35 GMACs end-to-end, ~54× less than AudioSep, FiLM on frozen DAC latents); CodeSep, arXiv:2601.12757 (identity lives in base tokens, never late RVQ layers). The efficiency play, not quality — no negative-SIR numbers, no phone-CPU RTF for latent-space TSE, quality ceiling below discriminative SOTA. (Cite arXiv IDs exactly — CodeSep ≠ CodecSep.) **58. Generative correctors (MeCo/DDTSE).** One-step mean-flow corrector on frozen discriminative output (MeCo, Interspeech 2026, arXiv:2606.09677; DSO dual-objective) or 2-step diffusion plug-in (DDTSE, SLT 2024, arXiv:2309.13874, RTF 0.139 — the only TSE-native paper with a real RTF table). Load-bearing rules: joint-train with supervised anchoring (StoRM precedent — α=0 collapses); identity gate *before* the corrector; monitor NSR + SIM, never SI-SDR/DNSMOS alone. The documented hard limit: correctors make wrong-speaker output *prettier*, and SIM can regress while SI-SNR rises — a high-perceptual-quality wrong-speaker render is the worst demo failure. **59. One-step flow TSE.** MeanFlow-TSE (Interspeech 2026, arXiv:2512.18572), AlphaFlowTSE (arXiv:2603.10701, mixture-anchored transport — MR prediction loses 24.8 dB on clean when ablated; at −15 dB the mixing-ratio coordinate is unobservable). 359M params, GPU RTF, Libri2Mix-only. Revisit triggers: a sub-50M one-step TSE appears, or the discriminative core needs perceptual polish. **60. Virtual-mic unsupervised learning.** UNSSOR's mixture-consistency collapses at 2-mic/2-speaker; VM-UNSSOR manufactures higher-SNR virtual microphones from classical demixers (10.7 dB, ICASSP 2026, arXiv:2510.08914). Demoted per the one-mic rule; what transfers is the design shape — **train with spatial supervision, deploy on one mic** (UNSSOR's 1-channel-inference-from-6-channel-training: ~12 dB).

### 61–63. Wrong-device / excluded (not ranked as approaches)

**61. Dual-mic/beamforming machinery** — MVDR five-mechanism recipe, AuxIVA chains, beam banks, reference-mic gating, sparsemax occlusion handling, TF-GSC front ends, LABNet, REDnet, FlexIO channel weighting. Demoted per the one-mic device correction; kept as post-demo archive. Transferable fragments: selective downweighting of bad channels; the "train spatially supervised, deploy on one mic" shape. **62. Visual/lip-reading modalities** — AV-PVQE's 46%→1.6% confusion reduction documents the ceiling being given up; only audio-only transfers kept (FCS confidence, AVHuMAR's MAR mechanism). Microphone-only is the product decision. **63. Foreground-VAD-as-selector** — listed at #31 as a *gated fallback*; ranked here as a *selector* it is the prominence-inversion anti-pattern (at −15 dB the dominant speaker is the interferer by construction).

## 2. Top 5 recommended demo candidates (build sketches)

### Candidate A — The identity-gated causal TSE core (recommended default)

**Architecture:** causal separator core at ≤1 GFLOP/s (TF-MLPNet+2F-class tiny topology or SkiM skip-memory skeleton; S4D as the streaming primitive if the recurrent path is chosen). **Conditioning:** TF-Map per-frame similarity cues + multi-layer FiLM injection at the coarse→fine handoff; frozen ECAPA-512 enrollment encoder (raw PCM, one-time capture). **Loss:** per-source mean-of-dB negative SI-SDR + ε-tSDR floor + asymmetric deletion penalty (α swept per SIR bin) + X-SepFormer chunk confusion reweighting + SC-TSE centroid consistency (λ≈0.1, CLS). **Data:** U(−15,+15) dB SIR via LKFS loudness-difference mixing; enrollment augmentation (noise/RIR β≈0.6, SSA, pseudo-speakers, enrollment span-masking θ≈30%); SPCL per-stratum curriculum. **Gate placement:** runtime identity gate (enrollment↔output cosine per 1–2 s + CSE residual correlation) vetoes adaptation/bank updates/pseudo-labels; subtract-recover retry on flag; pVAD soft gate for compute. **Measure first:** NSR and r_scr stratified by SIR bin on a frozen checkpoint — before any architecture change. If NSR at 0 dB is not near zero, nothing downstream matters.

### Candidate B — VoiceFilter-Lite-style filterbank fast path

**Architecture:** stacked log-Mel masking network (the Google on-device lineage), INT8, ~3 MB class; asymmetric loss penalising over-suppression; 130K-param personal-VAD gate running the extractor only on target-active frames; PVAD 2.0 enrollment-less fallback. **Conditioning:** FiLM from frozen ECAPA (FiLM beats concatenation while shrinking the model). **Loss:** VoiceFilter-Lite asymmetric L2 + mean-of-dB SI-SDR. **Gate placement:** same runtime identity gate as Candidate A (the gate is backbone-agnostic). **Measure first:** wrong-enrollment sanity row + NSR at 0 dB — the lineage optimises WER, so confusion must be measured explicitly. Fastest path to a demoable artefact; production lineage de-risks engineering.

### Candidate C — Denoiser-guided dual-path (D-LGTSE on pretrained GTCRN)

**Architecture:** frozen streaming GTCRN (MIT weights) → Y_d; enrollment↔Y_d context attention for guidance; extraction backbone masks the **raw** mixture; D-arm training (denoised-mixture augmentation). **Conditioning:** guidance decontamination is the mechanism — noise stripped before attention, distortion never baked into output. **Loss:** same confusion stack as Candidate A; front-end aggressiveness (Carson's dual-asymmetric λ) tuned per SIR bin. **Gate placement:** identity gate before any second-stage refinement; gate thresholds recalibrated because Y_d shifts embedding geometry. **Measure first:** guidance-weight quality (attention entropy on Y_d vs Y) and NSR at 0/−5 dB; verify the denoiser doesn't erase the quiet target at −15 dB before trusting the path there.

### Candidate D — Interferer-first masker + TSE-on-residue

**Architecture:** stage 1 — causal interferer mask estimator (no enrollment needed; the loud source is the easier estimation problem); mask-based suppression (not direct subtraction); stage 2 — frozen TSE baseline (Candidate A/B core) on the residue, enrollment-conditioned. Runtime variant first: Zhao post-filtering subtraction on gate-flagged confusion (zero retraining). **Conditioning:** negative-enrollment variant — condition stage 2 on the interferer estimate as "who the target is NOT" (Xu et al. Siamese comparison head). **Loss:** NASS-style mean-of-dB over C+1 channels so the loud interferer channel doesn't drown gradients; mutual-exclusivity penalty between branches. **Gate placement:** identity gate decides whether the residue path beats direct extraction per utterance (the falsification gate: half-day CPU oracle test first — frozen TSE on residue vs direct, at U(−15,0) dB; if the residue path doesn't win, shelve the direction). **Measure first:** the oracle test. No GPU burn before it.

### Candidate E — TFACM + enrollment-initialised identity bank (streaming memory)

**Architecture:** TFACM-Small causal core (cache-memory re-encoding) + MoMuSE/MeMo-style identity bank: enrollment initialises the anchor, updates gated by enrollment↔output similarity (never blind FIFO), separate speaker/contextual banks; dual-model cadence (fast extractor + ~1 Hz slow identity refresh) for long sessions. **Conditioning:** seeded attractor from enrollment embedding driving a frame-level target-activity prior; HyWA-style hypernetwork enrollment→VAD weight deltas for the always-on gate. **Loss:** confusion stack + predictive-SkiM/CPC auxiliary on memory states (zero inference cost). **Gate placement:** the bank *is* the gate's memory — admission threshold calibrated per SIR bin; rollback on judged-faulty admissions. **Measure first:** the per-SIR-bin CM-vs-bank ablation at −12 dB (predicted divergence point) and the momentum-poisoning threshold (at what SIR self-enrollment flips harmful). Post-demo upgrade path: Gated DeltaNet cell replacing the hand-designed updater.

## 3. Stop doing / deprioritise

**Wrong-device (one mic confirmed):** all MVDR/beamforming/AuxIVA/beam-bank/reference-mic machinery; dual-mic occlusion stacks; visual/lip-reading for the demo (AV-PVQE's 46%→1.6% only documents the given-up ceiling). Kept as post-demo archive.

**Disproven or anti-pattern:**
- Identity as an *optimised reward* — DDPO-VC's λ-sweep shows increasing a frozen speaker-reward weight degrades every metric; GenTSE's DPO with proxy-MOS raised perceptual scores while *reducing* speaker similarity. Gate, never reward.
- DNSMOS/UTMOS/NISQA as training signals or selection criteria — gameable (2.83→4.18 with TER unchanged; ~90% of utterances for NISQA); the REAL-TSE challenge wrote rules against exactly this. Diagnostics only.
- Ungated self-enrollment / blind FIFO bank updates — EvoTSE's τ=0 ablation: unguarded refinement is *worse* than no loop (NSR 9.2→14.3%).
- SpecAugment on enrollment — raises accuracy while lowering SI-SDR.
- Naive zero-shot-TTS enrollment augmentation — ZeSTA measured ~6.5% relative speaker-similarity degradation; park for the hackathon.
- Pooled-embedding-only conditioning at negative SIR — the documented bottleneck; EER doesn't predict TSE (SV rankings invert inside TSE).
- Mean-SI-SDRi-only evaluation — inflates from negative baselines and hides wrong-speaker extraction; NSR/r_scr/TSOS stratified, always.
- Energy-weighted loss aggregation (wSDR) — structurally down-weights the target exactly when it's quiet; the −15 dB trap.
- Separation-then-select as the demo path — MUSE's −1.3 dB collapse; keep as the baseline to beat, not the path.
- Hard-gating pVAD — costs ~0.7 dB and deletes real speech on false negatives; soft posteriors only.
- Quantized timbre tokens / codec-latent enrollment — identity tax measured three ways; tokens leak voiceprints (SpInv) while erasing usable identity (DECAF).
- Foreground-VAD-as-selector — the prominence-inversion anti-pattern (dominant = interferer at −15 dB).
- Naive causalization (unidirectional swap without state caching/norm audit) — documented multi-dB degradation; follow the checklist.
- MACs-based model selection — two published inversions; deadline histograms on the actual SoC.

**Citation hygiene (do not propagate):** DCF-Net (not IRA) owns the 0.4%/21.6 dB claim, and DCF-Net is a preprint; Malek's 0.3% FP rate is CHiME-4 speech–noise only (speech–speech: 5.5%/10.3%); cite DPMamba v2/proceedings, not v1; PLCPA-ASYM/TSOS is Eskimez et al., ICASSP 2022 (arXiv:2110.09625); EvoTSE's first author is Zikai Liu (not "Deng et al."); Serre et al.'s "100× tiny speaker encoder" is unverified — do not cite; PHA-RemixIT is provisionally located (Liao et al., ICASSP 2025) — verify the primary before citing; never rely on H-GTCRN's paper "Masking 1/2" labels (repo README warns they are used inconsistently — reference by mechanism); SonicSieve's venue is unaudited (MobiSys 2025 vs CHI 2026 claim) — audit before citing; SS-SENet is watch-only (no verified publication); CodeSep ≠ CodecSep (cite arXiv IDs exactly).

**Parked for post-demo:** generative correctors (MeCo/DDTSE), one-step flow TSE, codec-latent streaming masker, remixing loops without the identity gate, joint encoder+separator training, Kanagawa SSL→student distillation, k-NN VC interferer diversity, multi-resolution continuity regularizer (time the GPU cost first), AFW meta-learned updater (needs long-session protocol), virtual-mic unsupervised learning.

## 4. Notes on the ranking

- **Numbers are authors' as-reported** (mostly abstract/section-level survey depth); treat every dB figure as provisional until verified against full texts before any `research/` citation.
- **Nothing in the surveyed literature is evaluated at −15 dB SIR with enrollment conditioning on phone hardware, streaming.** The −15 dB column throughout is a literature-derived hypothesis, not a measured result. The asymmetric-SIR grid with NSR/r_scr per stratum is the single highest-value experiment the project can run.
- **No published Snapdragon/iPhone RTF exists for waveform TSE** — only VoiceFilter-Lite (mobile, quantized) and the denoiser family have on-device numbers. The phone-CPU measurement is ours.
- The ranking weights the relaxed minimum bar (correct selection at 0 dB) first. If the bar were still −15 dB, the interferer-first family (#8) and GTB&GTF (#5) would move up and the shipped-precedent family (#3) would move down.
- Dual-mic and visual survey lines (roughly a third of the corpus) were excluded from ranking per the one-mic device correction; their transferable fragments are noted inline.
