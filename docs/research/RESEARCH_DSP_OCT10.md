## 0. What the repo already tells us (do not repeat)
- `dfn` as post-filter is measured: on the isolated voice DNSMOS OVRL 2.78 -> 3.08, BAK 3.28 -> 3.95, SI-SDRi flat (+4.88 -> +4.97), but +0.5-0.6 s of true dropouts, because DFN eats quiet target onsets (`docs/BENCHMARKS.md` "Perceptual post-filter on the isolated voice"). T-195 repeats it: 1 s window hard scene SI-SDRi 4.23 -> 3.76 and wrong-voice proxy 8 -> 10% with dfn. `dfn_ll` fails (-1.28 dB). Skip a plain re-run.
- Raising the doubtful floor (K3, `HEARING_UNSURE_DB=12`) raises wrong-speaker time (BENCHMARKS T-180 section); confidence itself helps (5.2 vs 8.3% and 6.2 vs 8.3% wrong-speaker). So a deeper duck alone is not the lever: when the pick is wrong, ducking the residual plays the wrong voice louder.
- Code facts (read, `hearing/fusion/mixer.py`): one broadband residual gain per chunk, `floor = 6 + 34*c` dB straight from the window's confidence, every change ramped linearly in 30 ms (`SLIDER_RAMP_MS`), equal rise and fall, no hysteresis, no lookahead. A window flip 0.9 -> 0.1 moves the other voice by up to 27 dB in 30 ms. `hearing/enhance/dfn.py` already has an unused `post_filter` flag (beta 0.02, the DFN2 Valin post-filter).
- Neither MossFormer2 stream, nor any post-filter, can fix a wrong pick; T-200 says the pick matches the best stream 50/50 in the hard case. Everything below is about what the listener hears when the pick is doubtful, and about noise in pauses.

## 1. Post-filters after separation
**1a. Stream-prior Wiener / OM-LSA soft mask with a gain floor (replace the window-wide pick by a per-bin one).** Both MossFormer2 streams plus `mix - s1 - s2` give a direct noise/interferer PSD estimate, so the a-priori SNR is read off the streams and needs no minima tracking; classic IMCRA/OM-LSA tracking is the weak part in babble (Cohen's OM-LSA [sp_Nov2001](https://webee.technion.ac.il/Sites/People/IsraelCohen/Publications/sp_Nov2001.pdf); a 2023 SIViP study is reported to find IMCRA-OMLSA poor in nonstationary babble: [link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11760-023-02541-y), snippet only, not opened). Musical noise is the known cost of hard per-bin gains; cepstro-temporal smoothing of the gains removes isolated spectral peaks while keeping onsets and plosives ([Breithaupt/Gerkmann/Martin 2007](https://uol.de/f/6/dept/mediphysik/ag/speech/download/paper/breithaupt_gerkmann_martin_cepstralSmoothing_spl2007.pdf)); a gain floor of about -15 dB keeps a comfort-noise-like residual instead of holes.
- **Gain (source numbers, different tasks):** Wiener post-filtering of X-UMX music separation 5.13 -> 5.79 dB SDR at equal latency ([arXiv 2402.17701](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.17701)); mixture-consistency projection +0.7 dB SI-SDR (enhancement, [Wisdom et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.08521)) and +0.5 dB SI-SNRi on WSJ0-2mix ([MixIT supplement](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/28538c394c36e4d5ea8ff5ad60562a93-Supplemental.pdf)). The repo already applies a shared least-squares gain to both streams, so expect at most about 0.5 dB SI-SDRi; the real hope (untested here) is partial rescue on wrong picks: bins where the two streams disagree get gain about 0.5 instead of 0/1.
- **Hours:** 5-6 (STFT 512/128 at 16 kHz, ratio mask `|S_t|^2/(|S_t|^2+|S_o|^2)`, gain floor, cepstral smoothing; score with the T-206 oracle/real-mixer harness on `t141` easy+hard). **Risk:** medium; one extra STFT pair per window (CPU, under 5 ms/hop expected, untested), adds smearing if smoothing is too long. **Knob:** `HEARING_POST_WIENER=0` default; gain floor `HEARING_POST_WIENER_FLOOR_DB=15`.

**1b. DFN only where it hurts least.** DFN2's own post-filter "slightly over-attenuates noisy bins and gives gain back to cleaner ones" ([arXiv 2205.05474](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.05474)); that paper shows no with/without ablation, so the effect of `post_filter=True` is unmeasured. Untested here: dfn's dropout cost sits on confident windows (quiet onsets), its BAK gain on doubtful ones, so gate it on `c < 0.5` or run it on the residual path only. **Gain:** BAK +0.67 / OVRL +0.30 where applied (BENCHMARKS above); no source for the gated variant. **Hours:** 2 (flag exists; `enhanced_replay` already has the `+dfn` arm). **Risk:** low offline; live adds the T-075 CPU load on the GPU box. **Knob:** `HEARING_DFN_PF=0`, `HEARING_DFN_GATE_C=0.5`.

**1c. Learned post-filter on mixture + pre-separated estimate.** [Fan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07544): +64.1% relative SI-SNR, +25.6% PESQ over their pre-separation stage on WSJ0-2mix (relative figures; the abstract gives no dB). [Dual-Path Filter Network](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2106.07579) is a speaker-aware post-processor. **Hours:** 12+ (training); **risk:** high, different domain, and it cannot fix wrong picks. **Not recommended for tonight.**

## 2. Ducking and gain staging
**2a. Asymmetric, hysteretic floor with look-back/forward dilation.** Smooth the floor in dB, not in linear gain, and make it asymmetric: fast toward "more residual" (protect a possibly-missing target), slow (150-300 ms) toward "less residual", plus a Schmitt trigger on `c` (for example 0.4 / 0.6) so 0.5 +- noise does not flip 34 dB each hop. Log-domain gain smoothing with attack/release gives a smooth envelope without attack lag ([Giannoulis/Massberg/Reiss, JAES 2012](https://aes2.org/publications/elibrary-page/?id=16354), abstract summary; paywalled). Hearing-aid evidence: fast compression (about 50-100 ms release) in noise causes pumping and breathing, slow (800 ms-2 s) keeps listening comfort and rates higher in sound quality ([Kuk et al., JAAA](https://www.thieme-connect.com/products/ejournals/html/10.3766/jaaa.17127); the time ranges come from a review, [PMC7380331](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7380331), snippet only).
- **Lookahead:** the 1.5 s output delay (window + hop) means each output chunk lies inside windows that were already scored; take the min of `c` across the (up to two) overlapping windows and dilate doubt by 100-250 ms each side so the duck starts before the bad audio, like a lookahead limiter. I did not read the tse stage: check how `tse_confidence` is emitted around window boundaries before building this. No source gives a gain number; the measurable output is "dB swing per second of the residual gain" and wrong-speaker seconds, replayed with recorded confidences.
- **Hours:** 2-3. **Risk:** low (pure gain logic; a slow release can keep the other voice audible 0.2 s longer after a doubtful window). **Knob:** `HEARING_FLOOR_RELEASE_MS=30`, `HEARING_FLOOR_HYST=0` (current behaviour = default).
- **Click-free crossfade:** the current linear ramp is fine for gain steps (target and residual are correlated). For a hard stream switch (different voices, uncorrelated) a Hann/equal-power overlap is the usual choice (textbook, no source checked); the existing overlap-add already uses Hann weights.

**2b. AGC: gate level updates by the selected stream's speech activity, not the output level.** WebRTC AGC2 estimates speech level from frame RMS weighted by VAD speech probability, estimates the noise floor by minimum statistics, caps adaptive gain at 30 dB (initial 8), keeps 1 dBFS headroom and requires VAD confidence 0.95 ([gain_controller2.cc](https://webrtc.googlesource.com/src/+/main/modules/audio_processing/gain_controller2.cc), [agc2_common.h](https://webrtc.googlesource.com/src.git/+/02df2eb1de61994e4a02e4a5eb010726933ffa39/modules/audio_processing/agc2/agc2_common.h)). Our AGC already tracks a noise floor (10th percentile over 1.5 s) and has the opt-in floor rule; what it lacks is a speech-presence input. We have a better one than WebRTC: the target stream's own energy vs the residual's, and `c`. Hold the gain whenever `c` is low or the target stream is below the residual. **Gain:** no source number; T-169/T-179 baseline to beat is +4.5 dB median lift on quiet windows and 10% of quiet windows more than 3 dB above input (percentile 10, agc.py comment). **Hours:** 2-3 (replay `20261009T203531Z-2`, `211905Z-8`). **Risk:** low. **Knob:** `HEARING_AGC_SPEECH_GATE=0`.
- **Short-term LUFS instead:** EBU R128 short-term loudness is a 3 s sliding window, momentary 0.4 s, neither gated; gating (-70 LUFS absolute, -10 LU relative) applies only to integrated loudness ([EBU Tech 3341](https://tech.ebu.ch/docs/tech/tech3341.pdf), as quoted by search snippets: my fetcher could not read the PDF). A 3 s window is slower than our 800 ms release and cannot follow a speaker change; K-weighting would lightly de-emphasise room rumble. **Not recommended**: gain is speculative and the current AGC has more tuning behind it.

## 3. How the products do it (all vendor or press claims unless noted)
- **Apple.** Conversation Boost = beamforming mics + ML focusing on the person in front ([Hearing Review](https://hearingreview.com/inside-hearing/events/apple-announces-conversation-boost-for-airpods-pro)); it is a spatial front-talker focus, not identity. Independent Hagerman-method lab measurement (babble 65 dB SPL at -3 dB SNR): CB alone about 1.9 dB, CB + ANR 100% 3.55 dB SNR advantage; delay not reported ([Hearing Review evaluation](https://hearingreview.com/hearing-products/hearing-aids/otc/evaluating-apple-airpods-pro-2-hearing-aid-software-acoustic-measurements-and-insights)). Live Listen streams iPhone-mic audio, so it adds streaming delay. I found no official latency for any of the three features. Our +4-9 dB SI-SDRi at 1.5 s is a different trade: more separation for 100x the delay.
- **Phonak Sphere Infinio** (DEEPSONIC DNN chip): 10.2 dB SNR improvement at 0 degrees vs omni with maximum strength, 9 dB default, together with fixed directional mics ([Phonak infographic](https://phonak.com/content/dam/phonak/en/evidence-library/white-paper/infographic/PH_Infographic_InfinioEvidence-Spheric-Speech-Clarity-210x297_EN_028-2703-02_V1.00.pdf)). Latency: one user measured about 6 ms normal vs 15 ms in Spheric (AI) mode ([HearingTracker forum](https://forum.hearingtracker.com/t/phonak-spheric-mode-has-long-processing-delay/106659), anecdotal). **Oticon Intent**: DNN 2.0, "up to 12 dB noise suppression", no latency found ([Hearing Health Matters](https://hearinghealthmatters.org/hearing-technologies/2024/oticon-intent-hearing-aids/)). **Signia**: nothing usable found. Takeaway: hearing aids stay near 6-15 ms and none claims to pick one of two talkers; they reduce noise and trade it against naturalness.
- **Pixel Buds Pro 2**: Conversation Detection = own-voice trigger that switches to transparency and pauses media; Tensor A1 handles ANC and Clear Calling ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/audio/earbuds-airpods/google-pixel-buds-pro-2-are-here-with-a-custom-ai-chip-and-double-the-noise-cancelling-power)). It does not separate talkers. **Krisp**: on-device DNN about 15 ms ([TechCrunch 2018](https://techcrunch.com/2018/12/10/krisp-reduces-noise-on-calls-using-machine-learning-and-its-coming-to-windows-soon), noise-only era; background-voice mode figure not found). **Nvidia Broadcast**: no latency figure found.
- **Why hearing aids stay near 10 ms:** disturbance appears above about 15-20 ms in open fits, but that is own-voice comb filtering, irrelevant for an occluded ANC earbud; audio-visual desync is reported above 80 ms ([Goehring et al. manuscript, citing Summerfield](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/414900)). Our 1.5 s is only tolerable because the listener is not lip-reading (demo framing, not DSP).
- **Design lesson:** noise reduction in hearing aids did not improve intelligibility in any of three devices but all reduced annoyance, and the most-preferred one had the worst intelligibility ([Brons, Houben, Dreschler 2014, n=20](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4227664)). Our claim for the demo should be comfort/"I only hear them", and listeners must be asked that.

## 4. Psychoacoustics
- **How faint must the other voice be?** No source I found gives a threshold for "only hear them". What exists: Acceptable Noise Level (listener sets babble to the highest level they will tolerate while following a story) in n=221 normal-hearing listeners ranged -2 to 38 dB, mode 10-11 dB below the comfortable speech level; below 7 dB predicts hearing-aid success ([Interacoustics summary](https://www.interacoustics.com/hearing-aid-fitting/affinity-compact/support/acceptable-noise-level-anl-test), [Nabelek et al. 2006](https://www.thieme-connect.com/products/ejournals/pdf/10.3766/jaaa.17.2.6.pdf), via search summaries). So +10 dB is a typical tolerance for comfort, a weaker bar than "I only hear them". For competing speech, informational masking dominates and is worst for the same talker/same sex, with intelligibility plateauing below 0 dB ([Brungart 2001](https://scholar.usuhs.edu/en/publications/informational-and-energetic-masking-effects-in-the-perception-of--2/)): male/male at a weak duck stays confusable. Level matters little for distraction: the irrelevant-speech effect was the same at 60 and 75 dB(A) but fell steadily when pink noise masked the speech, and the authors suggest uniform masking noise may lessen the harm ([Ellermeier & Hellbrück 1998](https://pred.uni-regensburg.de/id/eprint/49466/)).
- **Faint room tone vs silence:** supported but thinly. 10 listeners, 20 TIMIT utterances in 5 noises at -5 to 15 dB SNR preferred a loss that keeps a residual-noise floor (threshold -20 dB) 70.0 / 66.5 / 70.5% of the time over MSE / TMSE / SI-SDR losses (equal 8.0 / 12.5 / 6.0%), because DNN suppression leaves unnatural residual noise ([residual-noise-control loss letter, arXiv 1912.03679](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.03679)); shaping the residual to a predetermined comfort noise beat soft-decision enhancement in an earlier test ([Interspeech 2006](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2006/shin06_interspeech.html)); telephony inserts comfort noise as standard ([RFC 3389](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc3389)). No test on ANC earbuds: with ANC the room is cancelled, so a hard hole in doubtful windows or pauses may read as dropout (untested here; this is the point to test).
- **4a. Room-tone bed + short test of the leak level.** In pauses and doubtful windows, mix a stationary noise bed shaped to the tracked room floor (AGC already estimates it) at -35 to -40 dBFS, crossfaded in 200 ms, so a lower residual floor is not heard as a hole and the leak is partly masked. **Gain:** Shin 66-70% preference (different task), masking per Ellermeier; **Hours:** 3-4 including a 5-listener ABX with residual floors 10/15/20/25/30 dB, bed on/off, question "can you follow what the other person says / does it sound cut off"; **Risk:** low-medium: the bed must stay inside the AGC's noise margin (10 dB over the tracked floor) or the AGC lifts it. **Knob:** `HEARING_ROOM_BED_DB` unset = off.

## 5. Ranked top 3
1. **Asymmetric hysteretic floor + lookahead dilation (2a).** 2-3 h, no model, replayable offline with recorded confidences through the real `Mixer`; removes the 34 dB swings the code makes on a window flip and starts the duck before a bad window. Knob default = today's behaviour. Success: wrong-speaker seconds not up, dB swing/s down, no new clicks.
2. **Room-tone bed plus leak-level listening sweep (4a), with the AGC speech gate (2b).** 5-7 h for both; answers the open product question (how quiet, and does tone beat silence on AirPods ANC) with 5 listeners, and directly attacks "AGC lifts noise in pauses". Both off by default behind env knobs.
3. **Stream-prior Wiener/OM-LSA mask with cepstral smoothing and a gain floor (1a).** 5-6 h; the only item that can partly rescue a wrong pick per bin; expected SI-SDRi gain is small (about 0.5 dB by the music/MixIT analogues), so adopt only if wrong-speaker seconds or leak improve by the T-180 bar (leak >= 3 dB better, wrong-speaker not up).
Not recommended: another plain `dfn` run (done), learned post-filter training (1c), 3 s LUFS AGC (2b). 1b (gated dfn) is a 2 h side experiment if a GPU-free hour is free.
